Read this before you cut metalThe files describe the shape of the parts. They are an educational design, not a tested one: nobody has run this exact engine. A rotating part that is printed, cast or machined badly can fail violently. Read the safety page and the materials chapter first, and never run a printed part.

The parts

The step number is the stage of the layer-by-layer build where the part appears. Rotor parts spin at up to 125 000 rpm: they are the ones that must be balanced.

File facts

  • Format: binary STL, units millimetres, right-handed coordinates.
  • Axes: the engine axis is +Z, from the air inlet (z = 0) to the exhaust (z ≈ 255 mm); +Y is up. Rotating parts turn about the Z axis in the positive (right-hand) direction: clockwise when you look into the inlet. All parts share one origin, so you can load them together and they assemble themselves.
  • Quality: every part comes from a boolean solid-modelling kernel, so the meshes are closed (watertight), manifold and have outward-facing normals. They slice cleanly without “repair”. The impeller and turbine blades are smooth-faceted; scale or re-export at finer tolerance if you need smoother surfaces for casting patterns.
  • Size: 14 MB for everything. The viewer on this page loads them directly.
  • Mass in the table is volume × the density of the listed material; the whole engine comes to about 2.4 kg without the stand.

Printing, casting or machining each part

Home 3D printing is great for checking that parts fit and for making casting patterns. It is not suitable for any part that gets hot or spins. The table says what to use instead.

Lost-PLA / lost-resin casting in brief
  1. Print the part (castable resin, or PLA with thick walls) at the scale that includes shrinkage and add sprues and a vent.
  2. Coat it in a ceramic slurry or plaster-silica investment and let it set.
  3. Burn out the plastic in a kiln or oven (follow the investment maker’s schedule; ventilate well; the fumes are toxic).
  4. Pour aluminium A356 (or have the casting made at a foundry) at ~720 °C into the hot mould.
  5. Break out, cut off the sprues, heat-treat to T6, machine the bores and faces, then balance.

Molten metal and a damp mould can explode. Casting is a skill to learn from someone who does it, not from a paragraph. For the turbine wheel, order the casting from a foundry.

Change the design yourself

The files come from a script, not from a CAD session. Every dimension lives in one table at the top of tools/build-stl.mjs: impeller diameter, blade counts and angles, can diameter, hole sizes, nozzle area, and so on. To make your own variant:

git clone https://github.com/nomram/cocajet.git
cd cocajet/tools
npm install                 # manifold-3d, three, katex
node build-stl.mjs          # rewrites ../models/*.stl and manifest.json
node section-svg.mjs        # redraws the cross-section drawing
node build-stl.mjs --check  # sanity checks (watertight, sizes, clearances)

Then reload this page: the gallery, the 3D builder and the simulator all read the new manifest. The physics model (js/engine-model.js) uses its own copy of the key dimensions, listed at the top of that file, so change both if you want the simulator to follow your new design.
