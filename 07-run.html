Things to try (failure scenarios)

The start sequence, step by step

  1. Starter on. The shaft is spun to about 20 000 rpm; the compressor already moves air.
  2. Igniter on, valve cracked. A trickle of propane (0.3–0.5 g/s) enters the primary zone; after a second or two it lights. A soft “whump” is normal; a loud bang means too much gas.
  3. Ramp. The controller adds fuel along the engine’s steady-state curve with a limited rate. The starter helps up to ~45 000 rpm, then fades.
  4. Idle at ~48 000 rpm: self-sustaining. The fuel flow it holds is the “steady” curve of chapter 5.
  5. Throttle: the controller targets a speed between idle and 115 000 rpm and adds/removes fuel to chase it, limited by EGT and by acceleration rate so the compressor never surges.
  6. Cool-down. Fuel off. The starter keeps spinning (at ≤ 22 000 rpm) until the hot section is under 125 °C, then everything stops.

Troubleshooting table

SymptomCauseCure
Hot startToo much fuel while rpm is still low and airflow weakLower the light-off fuel; start the starter longer before fuel
Hung startLit, but stuck at low rpm: the turbine can’t break evenMore starter assist; check for compressor rub or low fuel pressure
Wet startToo rich to light; unburnt gas collects, then ignites with a bangPurge by spinning with the fuel off
Flame-out on throttle chopFuel cut faster than the rotor decelerates → too leanKeep a fuel floor of ~50 % of the steady value (the ECU here does)
Surge / stallBack-pressure too high for the compressor at that speedOpen the nozzle; slow the acceleration; check for a damaged diffuser
Rising EGT at steady throttleFouling, a failing liner or air leakageShut down and inspect
